Printer axis skew measurement

Measures the angle between a 3D printer's X and Y axes, and between X/Y and Z with the cube, from scans or photographs of a printed hole-grid object. Produces a G-code correction. Images are processed locally in the browser.

Print a test object

Each object has holes on an exact 12.5 mm grid. Skewcal finds the centre of every hole, so hole size, flow and elephant's foot don't affect the result. Print it as exported, without rotating it, and with any skew correction turned off.

Photo reference

Reference sheet

Marker ring for photographs when no tablet is available. Print at 100% / Actual size and measure the two arrowed spans; printers rarely reproduce paper scale exactly. Use the dark-window version for light-coloured plates.

Slicing rules

  • Don't rotate or mirror it in the slicer. The three small holes mark the origin and the X and Y directions. The origin hole must end up at the front left of the bed.
  • Use your normal print settings. No brim on the plates: it makes the plate edge hard to find.
  • Dark filament is easiest. Light filament works if something dark is behind the holes when you scan.
  • To check a correction, print again with the correction turned on and measure it the same way.

Printing the cube

  • Add a brim set to Outer brim only, about 4 mm. A brim inside would leave a lip on the face that has to lie flat.
  • Snap the walls apart at the corner tabs afterwards. Lay every piece letters-up when you scan or photograph it.
  • Front and back walls measure X–Z, left and right walls Y–Z, the floor X–Y.

Capture images

Take at least two images with the object turned 90° between them. Neither a scanner nor a reference sheet is exactly square; turning the object reverses the sign of that error relative to the printer's, so the two can be separated.

For scale: 0.1° of skew is 0.17 mm of drift per 100 mm of travel. Four images (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) instead of two average out image-to-image noise and give an uncertainty based on the observed scatter.

Flatbed scanner

  1. Lay the plate on the glass near the middle, either side down.
  2. Scan at 600 dpi, greyscale or colour. Save as TIFF or PNG so the resolution is stored in the file.
  3. Turn the plate 90° on the glass and scan again. Don't rotate the image afterwards; the plate itself has to move.
  4. Optional: turn it twice more (180°, 270°) for four scans. The scatter between them then shows the real uncertainty.
  5. Open the Measure tab, choose Scanner, and add all the scans.

JPEG scans work too if the file keeps its dpi. Otherwise enter the dpi by hand.

Phone camera

  1. Show the reference on a tablet (below), or print the paper sheet at 100% and tape it to a flat, hard table.
  2. Lay the plate inside the window, first-layer side down.
  3. Shoot from 40–50 cm with the 2× or 3× lens, the whole sheet in frame. The 1× lens up close distorts too much for accurate scale.
  4. Use soft, even light and no flash. Check that no lamp reflects off the plate as bright bands.
  5. Turn the plate 90° on the sheet and take another photo. Repeat until you have four: 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°. Turning the phone doesn't count.
  6. Use the original files. Photos sent through chat apps are shrunk and lose the focal length.

With the paper sheet, absolute scale depends on the sheet printing at exactly 100%; enter the measured spans. With a tablet, measure its 100 mm bar once per model.

Cube walls (X–Z, Y–Z)

Separate the walls at the corner tabs. Each wall is then captured like the flat plate, lying flat on the scanner, tablet or sheet.

  • Every piece is labelled on its outer face: FRONT XZ, BACK XZ, LEFT YZ, RIGHT YZ and FLOOR XY. The arrow points to the top as printed. Front and back measure X–Z, left and right measure Y–Z, the floor measures X–Y.
  • Lay every piece letters-up. The raised letters must not touch the glass, and a consistent side up prevents flipping a wall between images.
  • Two images per wall, turned 90° between them. Turning combined with flipping does not reverse the reference error, so do not flip a wall within a pair.
  • Measure both walls of a pair together (four images) with Plate: Cube wall and the matching plane. Agreement between the two walls is a consistency check.

Tablet reference

The marker ring can be displayed on a tablet instead of printed. Open this page on the tablet, lay it flat and show the ring full screen; the object lies on the screen and its holes are lit from below. A display is flatter than paper and its pixel pitch is uniform. Published ppi values are rounded, so measure the 100 mm bar once per tablet model.

Set brightness high, turn auto-lock off and keep the page at 100% zoom. Clean the screen; dust under the object raises it. Enter the measured bar length in the Measure tab. Models marked "calibrated" have a corrected ppi value.

Method and limitations

  • Measurement. Each hole centre is located to sub-pixel precision. The centres are fitted to the nominal 12.5 mm grid with an affine model, which gives the angle between the two grid directions and a scale for each.
  • Hole centres, not edges. Over- or under-extrusion and elephant's foot change hole size but not position, so they do not bias the angle.
  • Reference error. The 90° turn separates the scanner's or sheet's own squareness error from the printer's. The report shows the amount removed.
  • Linear only. The result is the average angle over the 100 mm grid. Local position errors, such as belt or pulley irregularities, are not corrected.
  • Photograph scale depends on the reference's true size and on the object lying flat. A bowed object is modelled when the fit shows a significant bow.
  • Marker holes must be at least partly open. If they cannot be identified with confidence, the analysis stops instead of guessing, since a wrong corner reverses the sign of the skew.

    Apply the correction

    If your firmware supports skew compensation (for example Klipper's SET_SKEW, or Marlin's M852 when enabled), you can enter the measured angle there. Otherwise the G-code can be corrected: each X/Y coordinate is transformed by the inverse of the measured skew about the bed centre, arcs are converted to short line segments, and X–Z / Y–Z corrections shift X and Y in proportion to height. Z values are not changed.
